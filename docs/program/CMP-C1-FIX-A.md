### Item 2 — each part of a split sheet registers onto its own region (`CMP-SERIES-01` section 9 item 2, `CMP-ENGINE-01` section 5)

* **Why it happened.** The corpus' sheet E05 repeats itself: the right half of the A1 sheet is its left half with the labels `(L)` replaced by `(R)` (an adversarial design of the fixtures lane). No word is unique on both pages (anchors: 0), the phase correlation of the two masks is a view-against-view method and, on a part that has half the extent, put the second part at +33.39 pt (the left copy of the same drawing) instead of +1193.39 pt; the vector-line vote then refined the wrong shift. The measured failure: `area_a_pt` `[78.89, 3.5, 1221.89, 1680.5]` against the first part's `[45.5, 3.5, 1188.5, 1680.5]`, coverage 0.4773, 29 differences.
* **Changed.** `engine/child/registration.py`: `by_template` finds where the **whole** of B's ink (frame included, service zones cleared in both masks) sits inside A's ink by normalised cross-correlation of the 36 dpi masks (`cv2.matchTemplate`, `TM_CCORR_NORMED`); A is padded by 6 mask pixels so a part a rounding larger than the sheet has a place; a mask over `PHASE_MAX_PIXELS` is reduced by an integer factor first (the 180 MiB lesson of `by_phase`) and the vector-line vote then refines the shift with a radius widened to the reduction's uncertainty (`refine_by_lines(..., radius_pt)`); a template score under 0.5 is not a place (the registration is then not reliable, never silently phase). `dice(..., crop=True)` judges the agreement over the area the part covers, so a quarter of a sheet placed exactly is not "unreliable" for covering a quarter of the ink. `register(..., crop=True)` is passed `part == "split"` by `job.py`; a part larger than the sheet falls back to the old method. Constants (`TEMPLATE_MIN_SCORE`, `TEMPLATE_PAD_PX`) are in `PARAMS`, hence in the content key; `ALGO_VERSION` `cmp-engine/3`.
* **Result on the corpus** (V4 page 5 against V5 pages 5 and 6, `part="split"`, before the profile change of item 8): part 2 `transform` `[1, 0, 0, 1, 1193.39, 0]` (the key says `translation_x` -1193.39), `area_a_pt` `[1238.89, 3.5, 2381.89, 1680.5]`, coverage 0.4931, 2 differences; part 1 unchanged (`[45.5, 3.5, 1188.5, 1680.5]`, 0.5013); no overlap; the sum 0.9944. ### Item 4 — the tile headers (`CMP-VIEWER-01` section 12 item 1)

* **Changed.** `api/routers/version_pages.py`: `tile_response(content, *identity)` sends `Content-Type`, `Content-Length`, `ETag`, `Cache-Control: private, max-age=3600` (that order, like the portal-block crop); `streamVersionPageTile` and (through the import) `streamComparisonPairTile` use it. The tag is `W/"<32 hex>"`, a hash of the kind of tile, the id (`cpr_` or `ctp_`) and every tile parameter (`mode`, `level`, `column`, `row`, `layers` as named, `align_blocks`), plus a router-local epoch to bump when the pixels change. It is **weak** on purpose: the id and the parameters name the picture, not the bytes (a later renderer may encode them otherwise).
* **Why the id is enough.** A page profile is a frozen row; a sheet pair that has a result is `done` and `result_id` is written only by the `computing -> done` update (`series/repository.py` `_FINISH_PAIR`), its page profile ids are in the frozen columns of the table, and the result is content-keyed and write-once.
* **No `304`.** `contracts/api/v1/openapi.json` declares for these two operations the responses 200, 401, 403, 404, 422, 500, 503 and no `If-None-Match` parameter; no conditional answer is given and a request that carries the tag gets `200` and the tile (a test holds both facts). No contract change.
* **Tests.** `tests/unit/comparison/tiles/test_tile_response_headers.py` (8, the real routers on a bare `FastAPI`): the two headers on both tiles; the tag stable for the same request; one distinct tag for each id and each parameter (5 for the page tile, 10 for the pair tile); page and pair tags never equal; no `304`. **Mutations** M4a (no `Cache-Control`), M4b (no `ETag`), M4c (`layers` out of the pair tag), M4d (the profile id out of the page tag), M4e (`public`) red; green after restoring.
* **Known limit.** A pair that has no result yet answers `mode=b` with B's own tile (no transform, `CMP-VIEWER-01` section 4 item 1) and `overlay` with `404`; a `200` of the first kind would be kept for an hour although a later answer (B on A's canvas) differs. The screens open the viewer for a compared pair; the router cannot know the state (the port returns bytes only, `ports.py` is not this lane's).
* The BFF of the web client must forward the two headers (`credentialed-forward.ts` forwards them for the portal-block crop); that file is a web file and was not looked at or changed.

### Item 6 — streamed `decode_mask` (`CMP-VIEWER-01` section 12 item 3)

* **Changed.** `engine/mask_codec.py`: `_inflate_into` asks the decompressor for at most `OUT_CHUNK_BYTES` (1 MiB) from at most `IN_CHUNK_BYTES` (64 KiB) of input at a time and copies each piece into one `numpy.uint8` array of exactly the expected length, allocated when the first piece proves that the stream produces anything (a header of 40 Mpx over no body costs no 40 MB). A piece is never asked for more than one byte beyond what is still expected (`max_length` of 0 would mean *no limit* to zlib; the room is at least 1), so a hostile stream stops after the byte that proves it too long. The array is read-only like the view the one-call decoder returned. Every refusal and its reason code is kept (`body_corrupt`, `body_length`, the header ones, the caps); `decode_mask` also accepts a `bytearray` or `memoryview` without copying it.
* **Measured** with the VIEWER's tool, `PYTHONPATH=src AUDITMANAGER_LEGACY_PROMPTS_REPO=/nonexistent .venv/bin/python tests/unit/comparison/tiles/measure_pair_tile_cost.py`, three runs before and three after, on the 2A0-sized synthetic mask (1682 x 1189 mm at 113 dpi = 7483 x 5290 px, 39.59 Mpx a plane, the blob 187 352 bytes, the inflated body 39 611 520 bytes), Python 3.12.3, numpy 2.5.4, host load 2.6 to 2.8 on 8 vCPU, before: the tree of `858e6a52`, after: the tree of `43e59bd5` (the file under test is `engine/mask_codec.py`):

## 5. Limits, risks, and what the integrator should know

1. **Item 2 does not use the order of the matrix's split candidates.** The series handler calls `compare_sheet_pair(..., part="split")` and `comparison/series/handlers.py` is not in this lane's paths; the engine therefore decides by the content (the whole of the part's ink, frame included, inside the older sheet). That holds for any caller and for the corpus' adversarial sheet (halves that differ in two labels and the frames). A sheet that repeats itself with **no** ink at all that tells the halves apart would need the order: `compare_sheet_pair` would take `part_index`/`part_count` and the series handler (`_part_of(pair)`, one line, from `pair.parts["parts"]`) would pass them. Not done: it is outside the paths and no case in hand needs it.
2. **The split's ink coverage is 95.7 %, not 99.4 %**, since item 8 finds the frame: the strip where the sheet was cut lies in each part's margin, a service zone the comparison leaves out. The figure is true to the rule (the margins are not compared) and the series' `group_coverage` takes it as reliable. A screen that shows "100 %" for a split would be showing the matrix's text figure.
3. **`VersionPageProfile.vector_line_count` now counts the segments kept** (lines + rectangle edges + straight curve segments), +2.7 % on the real set and more than double on the corpus. The contract's field and shape are unchanged; its meaning is "vector lines of the profile". `counts.drawn_lines` in the profile's summary keeps the lines as drawn, and the scan flag still counts drawn objects.
4. **Item 4's headers** are for an immutable tile; a pair that has no result yet answers `mode=b` with B's own tile, which a browser would then keep up to an hour although the compared pair answers B on A's canvas. The viewer opens for compared pairs (not verified in the web code, which is not this lane's). The BFF has to forward the two headers for them to reach the browser (it does for the portal-block crop); `If-None-Match` is not honoured (the contract has no `304`).
5. **Item 7 follows PDFium's reading of the default configuration, not the whole of ISO 32000-1 8.11**: alternate configurations (`/Configs`), the radio-button groups (`/RBGroups`) and the `/Locked` list change what a user can toggle, not what is shown at load, and are not in the digest.
6. **Item 3's fixtures end the work and the batches a comparison test creates, and the work and batches that were live when the directory's first test began.** A test that leaves its own work live fails its teardown. The sweep at the start is for other directories' leftovers. No ingest test was found to leave any: the whole comparison directory was green right after `tests/integration/ingest` before the sweep existed, and again with it (below); the sweep is proved by seeded leftovers (section 4) and covers the gate's one-process order, in which directories before `comparison` (`access`, `analysis`, `api`) run first and were not run here.
7. **Stale bytecode can survive a same-size mutation made and undone inside one second** (the harness first showed it as a "restored run" that stayed red); every proof in this report was repeated with `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1` and the package `__pycache__` removed. The shared scratchpad of the CMP session held another lane's `mut.py` that took the place of this lane's once; its run only printed `REFUSED` and changed nothing; this lane's tools live in their own subdirectory since.
8. **A mutation test built its input from the mutated constant** (item 8, `MAX_PATH_STEPS` raised to 10**9) and used 6.5 GB for a few minutes of a 12 GB host; the process was ended by its PID and the test was repaired (`43f90aec`).
9. **Not changed on purpose** (the integrator's records): the matrix build stays in the worker process; the comparison operations answer whatever the module flag; `comparison_edits_limit` stays reachable only below the edge; the ZIP reader and `__MACOSX/` wait for wave C3.
