# Wave 47 — GO: what this programme can do about the ten steps, and what it cannot

**`R-45` put GO here.** `docs/program/dispatch/GO_PATH.md` marks all ten of the owner's steps
against the tree: **three were already done, four cannot be waved at all, three are buildable.**

## The four that are not work, restated so no stream treats them as work

**Steps 3, 4, 7 and 9 need a machine, a domain, a certificate and an API key.** No stream can
produce any of them. **A wave that treats them as work produces a rehearsal and calls it
readiness** — and `PA-01` criteria 1 and 2 have read *cannot be established* through four
certifications for exactly that reason.

What this wave does instead is make the day they arrive **short and certain**.

## Sub-stage A — two streams

| stream | lane | subject |
|---|---|---|
| `W47-GATE` | `gate-w47a` | the publication-readiness command (`R-46`), the default-credential gate (step 5), and `D-103`'s placeholder blind spot |
| `W47-PASS` | `gate-w47b` | the password policy (`R-48`), the forced first change, and the durable session register (`R-47`) |

**The boundary:** `W47-GATE` owns `infra/**` and the readiness command's own module;
`W47-PASS` owns `src/auditmanager/access/**`, the auth router and `web/src`. **Neither touches
`contracts/**` unless the confirmation field forces it — and if it does, that stream stops and
reports**, because wave 46 has just closed a reseal and a second one in two waves is `R-11`'s
shape.

## What `R-46` settled, and why it reads backwards at first

The readiness command **reports and registers; it does not block.** A check that refuses a deploy
is the stronger design and the owner chose against it **with the precedent in hand**: `D-72`'s
repair was correct and fail-closed, and it **took the owner's stand down for a day**, because a
configuration that had always been wrong stopped being survivable the moment the code got strict.

**So the command's output is a corpus of problems, and its findings become register rows
requiring a ruling.** That is its deliverable: not a green light, but a list somebody has to
answer for.

## What `R-48` settled, and the gap it leaves on purpose

**8 characters, contextual blocklist only, no expiry, and a seeded account whose sign-in works
but leads straight to the change screen.**

**`D-101` is the consequence and it is named, not hidden:** the literal string `password` is
exactly 8 characters, is not the login and is not the product name, so it is legal — refused only
at the forced first change, where it is the *current* password. **The one-line repair is recorded
and not taken**; the owner has been asked.

## Judging

Per the standing instruction: **a judge at the close of sub-stage A, and two cross-judges on the
merged tree before the final testing.**

**And wave 45's lesson about judging goes into their briefs.** Its two cross-judges converged on
the same finding and then noticed why that was weak evidence: *both had checked only where the
integrator's own commit trail made it salient.* **Two judges agreeing is evidence only when their
starting points were independent**, so wave 47's judges are given **different entry points on
purpose** rather than the same trail.

## The runbook is the integrator's

One artifact carrying `R-41` and `R-42` — *one person declares the pilot over, the same person
performs the wipe* — wave 45's measured rehearsal timings, and **every value the owner must
supply on the day, named.** It is not a stream's because it is the thing all three streams feed.
